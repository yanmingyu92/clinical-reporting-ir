# Adverse Event Summary Table – Data Quality & Signal Analysis

## 1. Internal Consistency Issues (Arithmetic Errors)

The "Total" column should equal the sum of Placebo + High Dose + Low Dose. Multiple rows fail this check, which is the most critical data quality finding:

| Adverse Event | Placebo | High | Low | Expected Total | Reported Total | Discrepancy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ATRIAL FIBRILLATION | 1 | 3 | 1 | **5** | 5 | ✓ |
| ATRIAL HYPERTROPHY | 1 | 0 | 0 | **1** | 1 | ✓ |
| MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION | 4 | 4 | 2 | **10** | 10 | ✓ |
| SINUS BRADYCARDIA | 2 | 8 | 7 | **17** | 17 | ✓ |
| DIARRHOEA | 9 | 4 | 4 | **17** | 17 | ✓ |

Most rows do check out, but the **percentage denominators appear inconsistent across arms**, suggesting different arm sizes. From the percentages we can back-calculate approximate N per arm:

- **Placebo**: 1 subject = 1.2% → N ≈ **86** (1/0.0117 ≈ 85–86)
- **Xanomeline High Dose**: 1 subject = 1.2% → N ≈ **84** (1/0.0119 ≈ 84)
- **Xanomeline Low Dose**: 1 subject = 1.2% → N ≈ **84**
- **Total column**: Denominators vary — e.g., 1 subject = 0.4% → N ≈ **254** (86+84+84 = 254 ✓)

The percentages are internally consistent with these denominators. However, several **"Total" column percentage values warrant scrutiny**:

| AE | Computed % (count/254) | Reported % | Match? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SUPRAVENTRICULAR EXTRASYSTOLES | 3/254 = 1.18% | 1.2% | ✓ |
| CHILLS | 3/254 = 1.18% | 1.2% | ✓ |
| PYREXIA | 3/254 = 1.18% | 1.2% | ✓ |

These check out. The arithmetic appears broadly consistent, suggesting the table was programmatically generated.

### 2b. Paradoxically Higher Rates in Placebo Than Active Arms

These are unusual and warrant investigation for potential confounding, differential dropout, or coding issues:

| Adverse Event | Placebo | High Dose | Low Dose | Concern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DIARRHOEA** | **10.5%** | 4.8% | 4.8% | Placebo rate >2× active arms |
| **UPPER RESP. TRACT INFECTION** | **7.0%** | 3.6% | 1.2% | Inverse dose-response |
| **ECG ST SEGMENT DEPRESSION** | **4.7%** | 0.0% | 1.2% | 4 cases in placebo, 0 in high dose |
| **EAR INFECTION** | **2.3%** | 0.0% | 0.0% | All cases in placebo |
| **URINARY TRACT INFECTION** | **2.3%** | 1.2% | 0.0% | Higher in placebo |
| **EXCORIATION** | **2.3%** | 1.2% | 1.2% | Higher in placebo |
| **HYPOTENSION** | **2.3%** | 0.0% | 1.2% | Higher in placebo |

> **Key concern – DIARRHOEA**: A 10.5% placebo rate that is more than double both active arms is highly unusual. This could reflect: (a) differential dropout in active arms before GI events could be captured, (b) a protective drug effect, (c) a coding or data entry error, or (d) chance in small samples. **This warrants source data verification.**

> **Key concern – ECG ST SEGMENT DEPRESSION**: 4.7% in placebo vs. 0.0% in high dose is striking. If the drug has cardiovascular effects (as suggested by sinus bradycardia signal), the absence of ECG findings in the high-dose arm while placebo shows them is counterintuitive and may reflect differential ECG monitoring, differential dropout, or a genuine pharmacological effect.
